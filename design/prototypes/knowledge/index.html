Design exploration

Knowledge, four ways

94 grammar topics · every topic gets a small rule sheet · four ways to navigate them

Each prototype is a working toy: real topic list, real levels, fake mastery dots. Six topics carry full rule sheets — try Imparfait vs passé composé, The pronoun en, Si + imparfait, Subjonctif after feelings, Futur proche or Negation.

01

Le Manuel — the coursebook

chosen ✓→

Levels are chapters, sections are parts, topics are numbered lessons. A table of contents you leaf through.

Best when knowledge should feel like a curriculum — ordered, finite, cover-to-cover. Lesson numbers give learners a shared vocabulary (“I’m on 2.07”).
02

Le Dictionnaire — search first

→

No chapters — a reference you consult. Alphabetical with a letter rail, live filter, and dictionary-style entries.

Best when learners arrive with a question (“how does en work?”), not a study plan. Zero browsing ceremony, one keystroke to anything.
03

Le Parcours — the path

→

One trail through all 94 steps. Mastery rings on a rail, level milestones, a “you are here” pulse, and a Continue button that always knows what’s next.

Best when knowledge should feel like progress. Ties directly into the practice coach — the path is what the coach walks you down.
04

La Table — the whole table

→

Everything visible at once: four drawers (Tense · Mood · Nouns · Sentence), each with a level-distribution bar, collapsible into dense rows.

Best when the catalog itself is the interface — you want to see scope (“where do I stand in all of this?”) and jump anywhere in two taps.
